Priv.-Doz. Dr. Yunteng WANG (王允腾)

Head of Research Group • Senior Scientist • Privatdozent (Venia Docendi)

Institute of Geotechnical Engineering, Department of Civil Engineering and Natural Hazards, BOKU University

Dr. Yunteng Wang leads the Computational Geomechanics Group at BOKU Vienna. His primary research focuses on Peridynamics (PD) formulations for crack initiation, propagation, and coalescence in rocks, Phase-Field modeling of brittle/ductile fracture and shear localization in saturated/unsaturated geomaterials, and multi-scale simulations of large-deformation geohazards. He was awarded the BOKU Best Paper Award (City of Vienna Anniversary Fund) and serves as Principal Investigator on several research initiatives.

Scientific Pillars

Core Research Directions

Integrating nonlocal Peridynamics, variational Phase-Field methods, and particle simulations to tackle critical challenges in civil and geotechnical engineering.

PD Mechanics [Core Focus]

Peridynamics (PD) in Geomaterials & Rocks

Nonlocal formulation for progressive crack initiation, propagation, and coalescence in heterogeneous rock and soil media without predefined failure surfaces.

  • Bond-level energy-based & non-ordinary state-based peridynamic formulations for rocks
  • Dynamic crack branching, fragmentation, and mixed-mode fracture under impact/blast loadings
  • Adaptive coupling between peridynamics and continuum methods (PD-FEM/PD-SPH)
Key Objective: Resolving discontinuous displacement fields and complex crack topologies natively.
Phase-Field [Core Focus]

Phase-Field Modeling of Fracture & Shear Bands

Thermodynamically consistent variational framework for diffuse crack propagation, strain localization, and multi-field coupled failure in porous geomaterials.

  • Thermodynamically consistent phase-field modeling of frictional fracture in rocks (IJP / CMAME)
  • Dynamic strain localization into compaction bands via phase-field approach (JMPS)
  • Coupled Thermo-Hydro-Mechanical-Fracture (THM-F) multi-physics in subsurface engineering
Key Objective: Energy minimization principles capturing crack bifurcation, merging, and frictional contact.
MOSCALA Project [FWF Project]

Progressive Failure in Scaly Clays & Geohazards

Investigation of macroscopic large-deformation failure and microscopic structural degradation in structurally complex scaly clays (FWF Funded).

  • Mechanics of fissured, sheared, and heterogeneous clay-rock formations
  • Large-deformation modeling of rainfall- and earthquake-induced landslides
  • Laboratory testing, CT micro-characterization, and constitutive modeling of scaly fabrics
Key Objective: Bridging microscopic fabric orientation to regional slope stability mechanisms.
SPH / DEM / SPH-FEM [Methodology]

Particle Methods & Multiscale Geomechanics

Advanced numerical discrete and particle continuum algorithms coupled with data-driven modeling for geotechnical hazards and granular flows.

  • Smoothed Particle Hydrodynamics (SPH) and Discrete Element Method (DEM) coupling
  • Solid-to-fluid-like transition in rapid landslides, debris flows, and avalanches
  • Data-driven constitutive modeling and scientific machine learning in geomechanics
Key Objective: Simulating post-failure runaway processes and high-consequence natural hazards.
